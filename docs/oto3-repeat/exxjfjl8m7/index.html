1 Customer Return System

Welcome Email Template

Subject: "Your [item] is on its way — plus a little thank you" Opening: Thank them by name for their purchase. Mention the specific item they ordered.

Middle: Set a delivery expectation (e.g., "Your [item] should arrive within [X] days"). Include care instructions specific to crochet items — hand wash in cool water, lay flat to dry, store folded (not hung) to keep the shape.

Close: Offer a first-look discount code for their next purchase. "As a thank you for being one of my customers, here's 10% off your next order. Use code WELCOME10 at checkout — valid for 30 days."

7-Day Follow-Up Template

Subject: "How are you loving your [item]?" Opening: Check in on their experience. "It's been about a week since your [item] arrived — I hope it's bringing you joy!"

Middle: Ask for a photo. "If you've taken any photos with your [item], I'd love to see them. I might even feature it in my shop (with your permission, of course)."

Close: Mention complementary items. "By the way, I just finished a [complementary item] that pairs perfectly with your [item]. I thought you might want to see it first."

30-Day Re-engagement Template

Subject: "I made something that goes perfectly with your [item]" Opening: Reference their original purchase. "A month ago you took home [item], and I've been working on something I think you'll love."

Middle: Show a matching or complementary piece with a short description. Emphasize that it was designed to pair with what they already own. Include a photo.

Close: Add light scarcity. "I only made [X] of these — reply to this email if you'd like me to hold one for you."

Monthly Subscription Model: The "Stitch of the Month Club"

Customers pay a fixed monthly amount and receive a surprise crochet item each month. This creates predictable income and keeps customers engaged.

Pricing: Set your subscription price at 1.5x your average item cost. If your average item sells for $20, charge $30/month for the subscription (the surprise factor and exclusivity justify the premium).

Sign-Up Message Template:
"Welcome to the Stitch of the Month Club! Every month, you'll receive a handmade surprise crochet piece delivered to your door. Each item is crafted exclusively for members — you won't find these in my regular shop. Your first item ships within [X] days of signing up."

"You can cancel anytime before the 25th of the month to stop your next shipment. No questions asked, no hard feelings. Already-shipped items are non-refundable."
2 Upsell & Bundle Templates

The Matching Set Bundle

Turn a single item into a 3-piece set. Customers get more value, you increase your average order.

Example: Beanie ($25) → Beanie + Scarf + Mittens set ($55, saves $20)

Template: "[Item A] loves company. Get the complete [Set Name] and save [X%]. Each piece is made in the same yarn and color to match perfectly."

The Gift Bundle

Wrap 2-3 items together with a handwritten card in tissue paper. Charge a 15-20% premium over individual prices. This works especially well during holidays.

Template: "Give the gift of handmade. The [Bundle Name] comes gift-ready — beautifully wrapped with a handwritten note. Just tell me what to write."

The Upgrade Upsell

Offer premium yarn or a custom color choice for an additional 30% on top of the base price.

Template: "Want this in [premium material, e.g., merino wool or organic cotton]? Upgrade for just [amount] more. Same design, elevated feel."

The Add-On

Pair a small, quick-to-make item with any order. Keychains, scrunchies, and coasters work perfectly at $5-8 each.

Template: "Add a matching [small item] to your order for just [price]. It takes me 20 minutes to make and ships with your [main item] — no extra shipping."

4 Review & Referral Playbook

When to Ask for a Review

3-5 days after delivery. The customer has received and seen the item, it's still fresh and exciting, and they haven't yet moved on to the next thing. This is the sweet spot.

How to Ask — Template

"Hi [Name]! I hope you're loving your [item]. If you have a moment, a quick review would mean the world to me. Just reply to this email with a photo and a few words, and I'll feature it in my shop. As a thank you, here's 10% off your next order: [CODE]"

5 Ways to Get More Reviews (Without Being Pushy)

  1. Include a handwritten card with every order asking for feedback. A small note like "I'd love to hear what you think" goes a long way.
  2. Share customer photos on your social media (with permission). When people see others being featured, they want to be featured too.
  3. Create a "Wall of Love" section in your shop. Dedicate a space to customer photos and quotes. It encourages new reviews and builds trust with new buyers.
  4. Send a gentle follow-up if no review after 14 days. Keep it short: "Just checking in — how's your [item] holding up?"
  5. Offer a small incentive. A discount code or free pattern download in exchange for a review. Keep it simple and genuine.

Referral Program Template

"Know someone who'd love handmade crochet? Share your personal code [CODE] and they get 15% off their first order. When they buy, you get 20% off your next order."

How to track: Create a spreadsheet with columns for referrer name, their unique code, number of times used, and rewards given. Generate simple codes like FRIEND-[NAME] so you can trace them easily. Update after every referred purchase.

Handling Negative Feedback

Not every review will be glowing. Here is a 3-step framework for turning complaints into loyalty:

  1. Acknowledge. "Thank you for letting me know. I'm sorry this wasn't what you expected."
  2. Offer to fix. "I'd like to make this right. I can [replace the item / offer a refund / send a different piece]. What would work best for you?"
  3. Follow up. After resolving, check in one more time: "I wanted to make sure everything is good now. Your satisfaction matters to me."
Full Response Template:
"Hi [Name], thank you for reaching out about your [item]. I'm sorry it didn't meet your expectations — that's not the experience I want for my customers. I'd love to make this right. I can [option A: replace / option B: refund / option C: send alternative]. Please let me know what works best and I'll take care of it right away."

SECRET The $0 Reactivation Email

This is one email designed to bring back customers who haven't purchased in 60+ days. No discount, no coupon — just emotion and scarcity. It works because it makes them feel remembered.

The Template

Subject: "I almost gave your piece away"
"Hi [Name],

I was reorganizing my workshop this week and I came across a [color/style] [item type] that reminded me of the [original item] you ordered back in [month].

I almost set it aside for someone else — but then I thought of you first.

It's a [brief description: e.g., 'chunky merino throw in that same warm caramel tone you picked last time']. I made [number, e.g., 'just three of these'], and one of them has your name on it. Not literally — but it could.

If you'd like me to hold it for you, just reply to this email. I'll keep it aside for 48 hours before I list it in the shop.

Talk soon,
[Your Name]"

Why This Works

  • Curiosity: The subject line "I almost gave your piece away" creates an immediate emotional hook. They have to open it.
  • Specificity: Referencing their actual past purchase makes it personal, not generic.
  • Scarcity: "Just three of these" and a 48-hour hold create urgency without feeling like a sales tactic.
  • No discount needed: The emotion and exclusivity do the selling. You protect your margins entirely.

When to Send

Target customers who purchased once (or more) but have been inactive for 60-90 days. Send it mid-week (Tuesday-Thursday), mid-morning. One email only — if they don't respond, move on. Don't follow up on this one; its power is in the scarcity.
