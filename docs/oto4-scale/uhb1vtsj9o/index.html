1 Outsource & Scale Playbook

Finding Crocheters You Can Trust

Where to find them:

  • Local craft groups and stitch-and-chat meetups
  • Facebook crochet communities (search "[your city] crochet")
  • Etsy sellers who already do custom work
  • Craft school graduates looking for freelance income
  • Retired crafters with time and skill

The 3-question filter:

  1. Can they follow a written pattern accurately?
  2. Can they match gauge consistently across pieces?
  3. Can they meet a deadline without reminders?

Start with ONE person and one simple pattern. Do not scale until that first relationship works smoothly.

The Test Project

  1. Send them your simplest best-selling pattern, the exact yarn, and the exact hook size.
  2. Ask for 2 identical pieces within 7 days.
  3. When they deliver, check: stitch consistency, gauge match, finishing quality, and communication throughout the process.

Quality Assurance Checklist (10 Points)

Score each point pass/fail for every piece you receive:

  1. Correct stitch count per row
  2. Even tension throughout the piece
  3. Correct dimensions (within 1 cm of your sample)
  4. Clean color changes (if applicable)
  5. Ends woven in properly and invisibly
  6. No visible knots on the right side
  7. Correct finishing (blocking, seaming as specified)
  8. Stuffing firmness matches your sample (for amigurumi)
  9. Safety eyes secured properly (for toys)
  10. Overall appearance matches your reference sample

A piece must score 9/10 or above before you accept it. Eight or below means rework or re-training.

Pricing Your Contractors

Formula: Pay 40–50% of your retail price per piece.

Example: Beanie retails at $30. You pay the contractor $12–15. Your profit per piece: $15–18 minus materials.

When to raise pay: When a contractor consistently delivers quality work and meets every deadline for 8+ weeks in a row, raise their rate by 5–10%. Reliable contractors are worth keeping.

Scaling from 1x to 10x

PhaseOutputSetup
Phase 11–2xOne contractor, your best-seller only
Phase 23–5x2–3 contractors, top 3 products

Move to the next phase only after you have consistent quality and reliable delivery at the current one.

Responsibilities: Managing customer messages, processing orders, coordinating with production team, updating inventory, scheduling social media posts. Requirements: Organized and detail-oriented. To apply: Tell me about your experience with e-commerce or handmade businesses, and describe how you stay organized when managing multiple tasks.

Test Project for VA Candidates

Give each candidate these 3 tasks that mirror actual day-to-day work:

  1. Draft 3 Etsy listing descriptions from your product photos and details you provide.
  2. Respond to 3 sample customer messages (provide realistic scenarios: a sizing question, a shipping delay, a custom order request).
  3. Create a weekly social media schedule for one product (7 days, platform and caption ideas).

  • Day 2: Walk through your shop platform, demonstrate the full order flow.
  • Day 3: Practice tasks with your detailed feedback.
  • Days 4–5: Handle real tasks — you review everything before it goes out.
  • Week 2 onward: Independent work with a weekly check-in call.
3 Digital Product Pivot

5 Digital Products You Can Create

  1. PDF Patterns — Write your patterns professionally and sell on Etsy or Ravelry for $3–8 each. No shipping, no materials cost, unlimited copies.
  2. Pattern Bundles — Group 5–10 related patterns into a themed bundle (e.g., "Baby Essentials Set") and price at 40% off the individual total.
  3. Video Tutorials — Record yourself making a piece start to finish. Sell access for $12–20. Film with your phone, edit with free tools like CapCut or iMovie.
  4. Printable Planners — Crochet project planners, yarn inventory trackers, pattern organizers. Design in Canva, sell for $5–10.
  5. Stitch Guides — Visual reference cards for stitch types, hook sizes, and yarn weights. Sell individually or as a complete set.

Converting Your First Pattern to PDF

  1. Write it clearly: Standard abbreviations + fully written instructions + stitch counts at the end of every row.
  2. Add photos: Include images at every key step where someone might get confused.
  3. Format it: Use a clean template with your brand header, consistent fonts, and generous white space.
  4. Save as PDF: Export from your word processor or design tool.
  5. List on Etsy: Write a clear title, upload 5 preview images (cover, detail shots, sample pages).

Materials cost: $0. Time investment: 2–4 hours. Potential income: Unlimited copies sold.

Passive Income Math

If you create 5 patterns at $5 each and each pattern gets 10 sales per month:

5 patterns × $5 × 10 sales = $250/month with zero ongoing work after the initial creation.

Every new pattern you add increases the total. Ten patterns at the same rate = $500/month. This income runs while you sleep, while you crochet, and while you focus on your physical products.

4 Wholesale in a Weekend

The Saturday/Sunday Plan

Saturday morning: List 10 local shops that could carry handmade goods. Think beyond the obvious:

  • Boutiques and gift shops
  • Baby and children's stores
  • Yoga studios and wellness centers
  • Pet shops (crochet pet toys and accessories)
  • Farmers markets and artisan fairs
  • Coffee shops with retail shelves
  • Hospital and airport gift shops

Saturday afternoon: Visit 3–5 of them in person. Observe what they already carry, note their price range, and pay attention to their customer base.

Sunday: Send a pitch email or message to all 10 shops on your list.

Pitch Template

Copy, customize, and send:

Hi [Shop Name], I'm a local crochet artisan and I noticed your shop carries [type of items they carry]. I think my handmade [your items] would be a great fit for your customers. I offer wholesale pricing at 50% of retail, with no minimum order. I can provide a sample set of [3-5 items] for you to display, and restock within [X] days of an order. Here are a few of my pieces: [attach 3 photos] Would you be open to a quick 10-minute chat this week? [Your name]

Wholesale Pricing

ModelHow It WorksBest For
Standard wholesale50% of retail — shop doubles your priceEstablished shops ready to buy upfront
ConsignmentShop takes 30–40% commission, you keep the restNew shops or hesitant owners — lower risk for them

Start with no minimum order to get your foot in the door. You can set minimums later once demand is proven.

Follow-Up Framework

  • No response in 5 days: Send one friendly follow-up message.
  • "Maybe later": Add to your calendar. Check back in 30 days with a new photo or seasonal angle.
  • "Yes": Deliver a sample set within 3 days. Include price cards and care instruction tags for every item.
  • "No": Thank them warmly. Ask if they know another shop that might be a good fit.
5 Done-For-You Pitch Deck Bonus

Build this 8-slide presentation to walk into any shop with confidence. Use Canva, Google Slides, or PowerPoint.

Slide-by-Slide Outline

  1. Cover Slide: Your brand name + "Handmade Crochet Collection" + your single best product photo.
  2. About You: 2–3 sentences about your story and craft. Example: "I've been crocheting for [X] years and I specialize in [your niche]."
  3. The Products: A grid of 6–8 product photos with names and retail prices below each one.
  4. Why Handmade Sells: Three bullet points — growing demand for artisan goods, customers pay a premium for unique items, zero mass-market competition for your exact designs.
  5. Wholesale Terms: Pricing (50% of retail), minimum order (none to start), restock time, payment terms (net 30).
  6. Best Sellers: Your top 3 products with one sentence about why customers love each one.
  7. Display Suggestion: A photo or description of how your products look best displayed in a shop — on a shelf, in a basket, or on a countertop stand.
  8. Next Steps: "I'd love to bring a sample set to your shop this week. Here's how to reach me."

Tip: Keep it visual. More photos, fewer words. Shop owners decide in seconds whether your products fit their store.
