1 Pricing-to-Profit System

The 3-Layer Pricing Formula

Step 1 — Base Cost: Materials + (Hours x Your Hourly Rate) + 15% Overhead
Step 2 — Wholesale Price: Base Cost (this is your floor; never sell below it)
Step 3 — Retail Price: Wholesale x 2

Set your hourly rate between $15–$25/hr depending on your skill level and market. Beginners start at $15; experienced makers with a following charge $25+. The 15% overhead covers hooks, needles, electricity, packaging, and Etsy fees.

Competitor Analysis (5 Steps)

  1. Find 10 sellers making the same item on Etsy or at local markets.
  2. Record their prices for the closest match to your item.
  3. Note the materials they use (acrylic vs. cotton vs. wool blend).
  4. Compare quality — stitch consistency, finishing, packaging.
  5. Position yours — if your quality is higher, price 10–20% above the median. If comparable, match it. Never race to the bottom.

Common Pricing Mistakes

  • Undercharging for time — your hours have value even if you enjoy the work.
  • Ignoring overhead — hooks break, patterns cost money, shipping supplies add up.
  • Racing to the bottom — competing on price alone attracts bargain hunters who leave bad reviews over $2.
  • Copying competitors blindly — their costs are not your costs.
  • Not raising prices — revisit pricing every 3 months as your skill and reputation grow.

The Window Light Technique

  1. Place your item 2–3 feet from a window with indirect light (no direct sun beams).
  2. Position a white foam board ($1 at the dollar store) on the opposite side to bounce light back and soften shadows.
  3. Shoot during mid-morning or mid-afternoon for the most even light.
  4. If shadows are too harsh, hang a white sheet over the window to diffuse.

Phone Camera Settings

  • HDR: Off (it over-processes textures and makes yarn look flat).
  • Grid: On (helps you center the item and follow the rule of thirds).
  • Focus: Tap on the item itself so the camera locks exposure there.
  • White balance: Set to "daylight" or leave on auto near a window.
  • Zoom: Never use digital zoom. Move closer instead.

5 Quick Editing Tips

  1. Brightness +15 — lifts the image without blowing out whites.
  2. Contrast +10 — makes stitches pop and adds depth.
  3. Saturation -5 — prevents yarn colors from looking neon on screens.
  4. Crop to center — remove distracting edges; keep the item as the star.
  5. White balance warm — a slight warm shift feels inviting and handmade.

3 Composition Rules

  • Rule of thirds: Place the item at a grid intersection, not dead center.
  • Flat lay with props: Arrange 2–3 small props (scissors, a plant, a cup of coffee) around the item at angles.
  • Lifestyle in-use shot: Show the item being worn or used — a beanie on a head, a tote on a shoulder, a blanket draped on a couch.
4 First 10 Sales Roadmap (14 Days)

Days 1–2

Set Up Your Etsy Shop

  • Pick a shop name (short, memorable, searchable — avoid numbers and hyphens).
  • Write your "About" section: who you are, why you crochet, what makes your work different.
  • Upload a banner (1200x300px) and profile photo.
  • Set policies: processing time 5–7 days, returns accepted within 14 days for non-custom items.

Days 3–4

Price Your First 5 Items

  • Use the 3-layer formula from Section 1 for each item.
  • Run competitor analysis for at least your top 2 items.
  • Write down final prices and keep a simple spreadsheet.

Days 5–6

Photograph All 5 Items

  • Shoot each item with at least 5 photos: front, detail, scale (next to a hand or ruler), flat lay, lifestyle.
  • Edit using the tips from Section 3.
  • Save at 2000x2000px minimum for Etsy's zoom feature.

Days 7–8

Write and Publish 5 Listings

  • Use the title templates from Section 2.
  • Add 13 tags per listing (Etsy's max) from the keyword list.
  • Write descriptions using the 5-part formula.
  • Set shipping profiles (calculate actual cost + $1 buffer for packaging).

Days 9–10

Share on 2 Platforms

  • Instagram: Post 3 photos (flat lay, process video, finished item). Use 15–20 hashtags from your keyword list.
  • Pinterest: Create 3 pins linking directly to your Etsy listings. Use vertical images (1000x1500px).

Days 11–12

Optimize Based on Stats

  • Check Etsy Stats: which listings got impressions but no clicks? Swap the main photo.
  • Clicks but no favorites? Adjust pricing or add a better description.
  • Favorites but no sales? Add a small "limited time" note or bundle offer.

Days 13–14

Launch Your Grand Opening Sale

  • Run a 10% off sale on all listings using Etsy's Sales and Coupons tool.
  • Share the sale in 3 Facebook groups related to handmade goods or crochet lovers.
  • Post a "shop is open" reel or story on Instagram with a direct link.
  • Send a message to 5 friends or family members who might buy or share.
